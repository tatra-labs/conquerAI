# Chargebacks and Disputes Policy
Effective: July 1, 2026 · Owner: Merchant Risk

This policy explains what happens when a cardholder disputes a payment made to your business, the deadlines that apply at each stage, and how to respond in the Merchant Portal.

## What a chargeback is

A chargeback happens when a cardholder disputes a charge with their card issuer — for example, because they don't recognize it, the order never arrived, or the goods weren't as described. The issuer reverses the sale while the dispute is reviewed, and MerchantBridge passes that reversal on to you.

When we receive a chargeback, we:

1. Notify you by email and in the Merchant Portal, on the **Disputes** tab.
2. Debit the disputed amount plus the chargeback fee from your next payout. If your payouts aren't enough to cover it, we debit your MerchantBridge business checking account.

## Dispute stages and deadlines

| Stage | What happens | Deadline, timing, or cost |
|---|---|---|
| Retrieval request | The issuer asks for information about a sale. Some chargebacks start this way; not all do. | Respond within 7 calendar days |
| Chargeback | The issuer reverses the sale, and we debit the disputed amount plus the chargeback fee. | Accept or respond within 10 calendar days of notification |
| Representment review by issuer | We submit your evidence to the issuer, which reviews it and decides the case. | Typically 30 to 75 days after we submit |
| Pre-arbitration / arbitration | If the issuer rejects your evidence, it can continue the dispute, and the card network may then decide the case. | Card-network arbitration fees, often $500 or more, are passed through to you |

> **Important:** The 10 calendar days are counted from the notification date, including weekends and holidays. If you don't accept or respond by the deadline, the chargeback is automatically accepted and the disputed funds are not returned.

## How to respond to a chargeback

1. Sign in to the Merchant Portal and open the **Disputes** tab.
2. Select the case to see the disputed amount, the cardholder's reason, and your deadline.
3. Choose to accept the chargeback, or respond with evidence.
4. Upload your documents and add a short explanation of what happened.
5. Submit before the deadline. We then send your response to the issuer — this step is called representment.

Retrieval requests also appear on the Disputes tab. A complete answer within 7 calendar days can resolve the issuer's question before it becomes a chargeback.

### Evidence checklist

Include whatever applies to the sale:

- Proof of delivery or shipment tracking
- Signed receipt, or the chip or contactless read log, for card-present sales
- Customer communications, such as emails or messages about the order
- Your refund or cancellation policy as shown to the customer at checkout
- A description of the goods or services you provided

## Chargeback outcomes and fees

- **If you win,** the disputed amount is returned to you.
- **If you accept or lose,** the disputed amount stays with the cardholder.
- **Chargeback fee:** $25.00 per chargeback, as listed in the Business Fee & Rate Schedule (code MER-05; it appears on your statement as CB FEE). The fee is non-refundable regardless of the outcome, even if you win.
- **Arbitration fees:** card-network arbitration fees, often $500 or more, are passed through to you.

> **Note:** Don't refund a sale that has already been charged back. The disputed amount has already been debited from your payout or checking account, so refunding it as well means you could lose the amount twice.

## Excessive chargebacks

If your chargebacks are above **1% of your monthly transactions for two consecutive months** — for example, more than 10 chargebacks in each of two consecutive months when you process 1,000 card transactions a month — MerchantBridge may place your account in a chargeback monitoring program, set a reserve on your payouts, or terminate card processing.

To keep chargebacks low, show your refund and cancellation policy at checkout, keep delivery records, answer customer questions promptly, and issue refunds to the original card rather than in cash.
