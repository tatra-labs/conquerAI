# PCI Compliance Program
Effective: July 1, 2026 · Owner: Merchant Risk

The Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS) sets security requirements for every business that accepts, processes, stores, or transmits payment card data. As a MerchantBridge merchant, you are responsible for meeting PCI DSS and for confirming your compliance each year in BridgeSecure, our PCI compliance portal.

## Your PCI obligations

All MerchantBridge merchants must:

1. **Comply with PCI DSS** for as long as you accept card payments.
2. **Complete the annual PCI Self-Assessment Questionnaire (SAQ)** in BridgeSecure by its due date.
3. **Never store full card numbers, CVV codes, or PINs.**

## Completing your annual SAQ in BridgeSecure

1. Sign in to BridgeSecure and check your SAQ due date.
2. Describe how your business accepts cards, such as BridgeTerminal devices, online checkout, or invoices. Your answers determine which SAQ applies to you.
3. Answer every question in the questionnaire.
4. Submit the completed SAQ before the due date.

The SAQ is due again every year, so plan to complete it annually.

## SAQ P2PE for terminal-only merchants

Point-to-point encryption (P2PE) encrypts card data inside the terminal as soon as the card is read. If you accept cards **only** through MerchantBridge terminals that use point-to-point encryption, you may qualify for SAQ P2PE, a shorter questionnaire. If you also accept card payments online or by invoice, SAQ P2PE does not apply, and you complete the SAQ that matches your full setup.

## Protecting card data

Never store full card numbers, CVV codes (the security code printed on the card), or PINs, whether on paper, in spreadsheets or files, in email or text messages, or in photos. In practice:

- Enter card details only at the time of the sale, and don't write them down.
- If you need to identify a card in your records, keep only the last four digits.
- Never ask customers to email or text their card details, or to tell you their PIN.
- Check your BridgeTerminal devices regularly for signs of tampering, such as unfamiliar attachments or damage, and stop using any terminal that looks altered.
- Give each person who uses the Merchant Portal their own sign-in, and never share passwords.
