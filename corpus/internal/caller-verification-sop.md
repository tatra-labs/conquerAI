# Caller Identity Verification Procedure
Effective: February 1, 2026 · Owner: Contact Center Operations · Internal — agents only

Before you discuss account details with a caller, confirm that the caller is an authorized user on the account. Verification establishes who the caller is; the caller's role on the account then decides what you can discuss or do.

## When to verify

Verify the caller before you discuss or act on anything specific to their account, such as balances, transactions, statements, payouts, cards, or account settings. General questions that do not involve a specific account, such as how a plan works or what a published fee is, can be answered without verification.

## Verification steps

Before you discuss account details, verify the caller with these three steps, in order:

1. Confirm that the caller's full name matches an authorized user on the account.
2. Ask for the business legal name and the last 4 digits of the business's EIN.
3. Send the in-app "Verify this call" push prompt and ask the caller to approve it in the MBFS app.

> **Note:** The business legal name and EIN digits are semi-public, because the EIN appears on W-9 forms. They show that the caller knows the business, not who the caller is, so the in-app "Verify this call" prompt is the primary check.

### If the caller cannot use the app

If the caller cannot approve the prompt in the app, ask for the amount of one transaction on the account from the last 30 days. The amount must match that transaction to within $1. Using this check because the caller cannot use the app does not count as a failed attempt.

### If the caller is not an authorized user

If the caller is not an authorized user on the account, such as an outside bookkeeper or accountant who has not been added, you cannot verify them and must not discuss the account. An owner or admin can add them as a user in the app; after that, verify them like any other user.

## Information you must never ask for

Never ask a caller for a password, PIN, full card number, or one-time passcode, whether to verify them or for any other reason. Customers are told that MBFS will never ask for these. If a caller starts to read one out, stop them and do not record it in the case.

## When verification fails

After two failed verification attempts:

1. Stop the verification.
2. Do not disclose anything about the account, including which detail did not match.
3. Transfer the call to the Fraud team.
4. Record the failed attempts and the transfer in the case.

## What each role can access

Once the caller is verified, check their role on the account and stay within it.

| Role | What you can discuss or do for them | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| Owner or admin | Full access to the account, including adding users and changing the payout destination | Changes to the payout bank account, contact email or phone, or users must be approved in the app, never over the phone |
| Authorized signer | Can make transactions and close the account | Cannot approve changes to the payout bank account, contact details or users, because those need an owner or admin |
| Employee cardholder | Their own card only: lock it, replace it, and review recent transactions on that card | No account balances, and nothing beyond their own card |
| View-only user (such as a bookkeeper or accountant) | You can share account balances, statements and transaction history after verification | Cannot make changes |

## Changes that always need in-app approval

These changes always require approval in the app by an owner or admin, even when the caller is verified:

- Changes to the payout bank account (where payouts are sent)
- Changes to the contact email address or phone number
- Changes to users, such as adding or removing a user

Never complete these changes over the phone, even for a verified owner or admin. Explain that the change must be approved in the app by an owner or admin, and do not make it on the caller's behalf. These changes control where money goes and who can act on the account, so they are approved in the app rather than on a call. If you suspect account takeover, transfer the caller to the Fraud team.

## Recording verification

Note the verification method in the case record: the in-app "Verify this call" prompt, or the transaction-amount check when the caller could not use the app.
