One question, end to end

Pick a question. These are real traces from the evaluation run, stage by stage: what the question was read as, what retrieval nominated, how the cross-encoder scored it, which source the structure rules chose and why, which gate rule decided, and the cited answer.

How it decides

Retrieval finds candidates and a small cross-encoder ranks them, but a relevance score cannot tell "incoming" from "outgoing", a 2027 notice from today's fee, or $30,000 from a tier range. So the ranker chooses what is relevant, structure chooses the value, a stronger cross-encoder checks that the unit about to be quoted answers the question, and an ordered list of named rules decides whether to answer at all.

The pipeline

Validity is applied before retrieval, so superseded content can never be a candidate.

flowchart TD
  Q[Agent question] --> A["Read: mask card numbers, strip filler,
KB glossary, account intent, dates, plans, amounts, sub-questions"]
  V["Validity on the date:
current / upcoming / superseded"] --> R
  A --> R["Retrieve: BM25 body + headings,
dense bge-small; union of 12 + 12"]
  R --> X["Ranker MiniLM-L6:
orders candidates"]
  X --> E["Evidence: trust tiers, fit check,
rows by structure, sentence runs, gaps, notes"]
  E --> W["Verifier bge-reranker-v2-m3:
does the quoted unit answer it?"]
  W --> G{"Gate: first matching rule"}
  G -->|answer / partial| T["Extractive answer:
every sentence cited"]
  G -->|clarify / refuse| N["Question or route,
never an amount"]
  T -.optional.-> C["Claude: cited per sentence,
values checked, never raises the decision"]

The gate, in order

The first rule that matches decides. Its name is in every answer as confidence.rule.

    Levels are actions for the agent: high read it out, medium check the source first, low share only what is cited. The number beside them is a raw cross-encoder logit, never shown as a percentage.

    What changed, and why

    The design moved whenever a measurement said it was wrong. In order:

    1. A corpus built to fail naively29 documents in markdown, HTML and CSV, with the ways real knowledge bases go wrong planted on purpose: a superseded fee schedule, an unapproved draft, an approved notice of 2027 fees, a stale FAQ, a help article that drifted from policy, a hidden A/B variant, near-miss products and internal procedures. Each trap has a test that proves it is really there.
    2. RRF out of the main pathWith a cross-encoder downstream, fusion only decides which candidates get scored, and reciprocal-rank fusion demoted exact BM25 hits on fee names that the dense side missed. Both retrievers now nominate 12 and the cross-encoder scores the union. RRF stays as the no-reranker ablation.
    3. Trust tiers, after the notice outranked the fee rowThe 2027 notice scored 9.46 against the current fee row's 8.26 for "What's the monthly fee for Bridge Essentials?", and the stale FAQ outranks the wire policy. "Authority, then recency" would have served next year's price as today's. Now the window is anchored on the best current, not-outdated source, and upcoming or outdated content becomes a dated or conflict note.
    4. A fit check, after the PCI row answered "monthly fee"Each candidate within 3 logits of the anchor pays 2 logits per question term it does not cover, judged on a row's label rather than its unit, plus 2 more if it states no value of the kind asked for. That fixed the monthly-fee and wire-cost questions without touching the scores.
    5. Rows by structure, because the reranker cannot separate siblings"Incoming" and "outgoing" wire rows score within a fraction of a logit in every serialization tried. Facet words derived from the rows themselves pick the row, plan names pick the column, and interval lookup picks a savings tier.
    6. No spell-correctorCorrecting against the corpus turned "cashier's" into "casher" and erased the signal that refuses the cashier's-check question. Only words the corpus has never seen get one-edit tolerance, and only inside the cited evidence.
    7. Calibration on synthetic questions, after the first attempt refused nearly everythingUsing gold-deleted dev questions as negatives produced floors that refused most answerable questions: with the right passage gone, an on-topic distractor still scores high. The floors now come from 205 questions templated from the corpus against 28 off-topic ones, and the gold-deleted measurement stays as the strip plot below.
    8. Code review, round one: five lenses, every finding reproduced by a skepticIt found that concurrent API requests shared per-request state (2 of 64 threaded answers quoted another question), that the scorer passed a wrong FDIC answer because a tier boundary of $250,000 matched the fact, that a year in the question hid most of the knowledge base, that card numbers with a CVV attached leaked, and that the LLM path let the model upgrade the gate's decision. All fixed, with tests.
    9. Code review, round two: the fixes, reviewed the same wayA notice's bullets were scored with the previous bullet prepended, so the paper-statement change answered an Instant Payout question; list items now read with their list's lead-in instead. A past-dated fee question could be answered by a policy that took effect later, so a fee or rate for a past date now comes only from sources in effect then. Card numbers split by a Unicode minus or zero-width characters were assembled by normalisation after masking, so masking now runs on normalised text and again after filler is stripped. The API's error body echoed rejected input. Claude's bullets were not separate claims.
    10. Held-out v1, blind: 3 of 12Twelve questions written from the brief's requirements by an agent that never saw the corpus design, labeled blind against the corpus text by another, frozen by hash and run once. Dev said 22 of 27; the blind set said 3 of 12. The gap was the finding. Four answerable questions were refused although their right passage ranked first, because the floor had been fitted on questions templated from the documents' own headings. Those score far higher than anything an agent types.
    11. Fixing causes, not questionsThe floors were refitted on a realistic calibration set: about 200 questions in shorthand, customer words, narratives and typos, written from the corpus alone and verified, with nothing overlapping a test question. The fit uses the score the gate actually tests. Also, an outdated source no longer supplies a value, a statement after a question is read as its context, and the account rules understand "this cust's". Every change is general; none names a test question.
    12. A verifier, because no floor could fix the rankerEven on realistic questions, the best floor for the small ranker refused about two thirds of answerable ones: it scores real phrasing and not-covered questions in KB vocabulary alike (AUC 0.87). bge-reranker-v2-m3 separates them at 0.95 on the same calibration questions, but costs about ten times as much per pair. It now scores only the unit about to be quoted, and the gate tests its score.
    13. Held-out v2, blind, onceTwenty fresh questions from two new writers and two blind labelers, under the same protocol, frozen by hash and run a single time after the fixes. That is the number to trust. Held-out v1, rerun after the fixes, is shown too, marked as no longer blind.
